# Translation Quality Guide

## Purpose

This guide provides concrete examples of high-quality vs low-quality translations for 红楼梦, helping ensure consistency across all translators.

---

## Core Principles

### 1. This is World-Class Literature

红楼梦 is not:
- ❌ A simple story for children
- ❌ Pulp fiction
- ❌ A historical document
- ❌ Casual reading

红楼梦 is:
- ✅ One of world literature's greatest masterpieces
- ✅ Psychologically sophisticated
- ✅ Symbolically layered
- ✅ Linguistically masterful
- ✅ Culturally profound

**Implication**: Every word deserves careful attention. Rushed translations dishonor the original.

### 2. Completeness is Non-Negotiable

**Rule**: Translate EVERY visible element on the page.

This includes:
- Main narrative text
- ALL poetry
- ALL dialogue
- ALL commentary (眉批, 夹批, 侧批)
- Chapter titles (if present)

**Common failure**: Translating only the main text and skipping commentary.

**Why commentary matters**: The 脂评 commentary often reveals hidden meanings and authorial intent. It's scholarly annotation that adds value.

### 3. Preserve Literary Beauty

**Balance required**: Accuracy + Literary quality

Example:

**Original**: 满纸荒唐言，一把辛酸泪。

❌ **Too literal (loses beauty)**:  
English: "Full page absurd words, one handful bitter tears."

❌ **Too free (loses accuracy)**:  
English: "Everything written here is nonsense, but I cried a lot while writing it."

✅ **Balanced (preserves both)**:  
English: "Pages full of idle words, a handful of bitter tears."

**Why balanced version works**:
- "Idle words" captures the self-deprecating tone of 荒唐言
- "Handful of bitter tears" preserves the literary measure word structure
- Maintains the conciseness and rhythm
- Accurate to meaning, beautiful in expression

## Good vs Bad Examples

### Example 1: Prose Translation

**Original**: 士隐意欲也跟了过去，方举步时，忽听一声霹雳，有若山崩地陷。

#### Modern Chinese (简体)

❌ **Bad**: 士隐想跟着走，刚要走的时候，突然听到一声雷，好像很大的声音。

**Problems**:
- "很大的声音" is weak, loses the imagery
- Missing the drama of "山崩地陷"
- Too colloquial

✅ **Good**: 士隐也想要跟着过去，刚举步的时候，忽然听见一声霹雳，好像山崩地陷一般。

**Why good**:
- Preserves classical rhythm with modern vocabulary
- "山崩地陷" kept for dramatic impact
- Natural modern Chinese while retaining literary quality

#### English

❌ **Bad**: Shiyin wanted to follow, but when he was about to walk, he heard thunder that sounded very loud.

**Problems**:
- "very loud" is weak
- Loses the apocalyptic imagery
- Awkward phrasing ("when he was about to walk")

✅ **Good**: Shiyin wished to follow them, but just as he was about to step forward, he suddenly heard a thunderclap, as if mountains were crumbling and the earth was caving in.

**Why good**:
- "Thunderclap" more literary than "thunder"
- Translates the full imagery: "mountains crumbling and earth caving in"
- Smooth, literary English

#### Russian

❌ **Bad**: Шиинь хотел пойти следом, но когда он шагнул, он услышал очень громкий гром.

**Problems**:
- "очень громкий гром" (very loud thunder) is flat
- Loses imagery
- Not literary register

✅ **Good**: Шиинь хотел последовать за ними, но едва он сделал шаг, как вдруг раздался удар грома, словно горы рушились и земля разверзалась.

**Why good**:
- "разверзалась" (разверзаться = to open up, gape) is literary
- Full imagery preserved
- Appropriate literary register for classical literature

#### Japanese

❌ **Bad**: 士隠は後を追いたかったが、歩こうとした時、大きな雷の音が聞こえた。

**Problems**:
- Too modern and plain
- "大きな雷の音" (big thunder sound) lacks literary quality
- Imagery lost

✅ **Good**: 士隠も後を追おうとして、まさに足を踏み出そうとした時、突然雷鳴が轟き、山が崩れ地が陥没するかのようであった。

**Why good**:
- Uses classical-influenced construction (〜ようとして)
- "轟き" (とどろき - thunderous roar) is more literary
- Full imagery preserved with appropriate vocabulary

### Example 2: Poetry Translation

**Original** (7-character quatrain):
```
世人都晓神仙好，惟有功名忘不了！
古今将相在何方？荒冢一堆草没了。
```

This is from the famous "好了歌" (Won-Done Song).

#### Modern Chinese

❌ **Bad**: 世人都知道神仙好，但是放不下功名利禄。古代的将军和宰相现在在哪里？他们都在坟墓里，长满了草。

**Problems**:
- Too prosaic, reads like prose
- "放不下功名利禄" adds extra words not in original
- Lost the poetic structure
- "他们都在坟墓里" is explanatory rather than evocative

✅ **Good**: 世人都知道神仙逍遥美好，只有功名利禄忘不掉！古往今来的将相都在哪里呢？只剩下一堆荒坟，被野草淹没了。

**Why good**:
- Maintains rhythm
- Preserves the rhetorical questions
- "野草淹没" is poetic, not prosaic
- Accessible but retains literary quality

#### English

❌ **Bad**: Everyone knows immortals are good, but they can't forget fame and status. Where are all the ancient generals and prime ministers? They're all in graves covered with grass.

**Problems**:
- No poetic rhythm
- "They're all in graves" is flat
- Doesn't feel like poetry

✅ **Good**: 
```
Men all know that salvation should be won,
But with ambition they won't have done, have done.
Where are the famous ones of days gone by?
In grassy graves they lie now, every one.
```

**Why good**:
- Creates rhythm in English (not forced rhyme)
- "have done, have done" echoes the original's emphasis
- "grassy graves" more poetic than "graves with grass"
- Feels like poetry to English readers

#### Russian

❌ **Bad**: Все люди знают что боги хорошие, но не могут забыть славу. Где древние генералы? Они в могилах с травой.

**Problems**:
- Grammatically poor
- No poetic structure
- "боги хорошие" is wrong tone
- Doesn't scan as poetry

✅ **Good**:
```
Все люди знают: быть святым — прекрасно,
Но славу позабыть они не властны.
Где полководцы древности, визири?
Травой могилы заросли ужасно.
```

**Why good**:
- Creates rhyme in Russian (прекрасно/властны, визири/ужасно)
- Appropriate literary register
- "не властны" (not able/powerless) is more poetic than "не могут"
- Scans as poetry

#### Japanese

❌ **Bad**: 世の人は神仙が良いと知っているが、功名を忘れられない。昔の将軍や大臣はどこにいる？草が生えた墓にいる。

**Problems**:
- Too modern and prosaic
- No classical influence
- "草が生えた墓にいる" is flat

✅ **Good**:
```
世人（せじん）都（すべ）て曉（さと）る神仙の好（よ）きを、
惟（た）だ功名有りて忘れ了（お）えず！
古今の将相（しょうしょう）何（いず）くにか在る？
荒冢（こうちょう）一堆（いったい）、草没し了（お）わんぬ。
```

**Why good**:
- Uses classical-style reading (音読み)
- "了えず" echoes the "了" theme of the original
- Appropriate vocabulary (将相, 荒冢)
- Feels poetic to Japanese readers familiar with classical literature

### Example 3: Commentary Translation

**Original**: 八个字屈死多少英雄？屈死多少忠臣孝子？屈死多少仁人志士？屈死多少词客骚人？

This is commentary on "有命无运，累及爹娘" (8 characters).

#### Modern Chinese

❌ **Bad**: 这八个字害死了很多人，包括英雄、忠臣、孝子、好人、诗人等等。

**Problems**:
- Loses the rhetorical power of repetition
- "等等" (etc.) is lazy
- "害死了很多人" is too general

✅ **Good**: 这八个字屈死了多少英雄？屈死了多少忠臣孝子？屈死了多少仁人志士？屈死了多少词客骚人？

**Why good**:
- Preserves the repetitive rhetorical questions
- "屈死" (wrongfully killed) is more precise than "害死"
- Maintains the rhythm and emotional impact

#### English

❌ **Bad**: These eight words killed many people: heroes, loyal officials, good children, and poets.

**Problems**:
- Lost the rhetorical structure
- "killed many people" is too simple
- No emotional resonance

✅ **Good**: These eight characters—how many heroes have they wrongfully doomed? How many loyal ministers and filial sons? How many benevolent and righteous men? How many poets and literati?

**Why good**:
- Preserves the rhetorical questions
- "wrongfully doomed" captures 屈死
- Creates parallel structure in English
- Maintains emotional impact

## Specific Quality Criteria by Translation Aspect

### 1. Name Translation

**Rule**: Transliterate, then explain puns in `notes`.

| Original | Modern Chinese | English | Russian | Japanese | Notes |
|----------|----------------|---------|---------|----------|-------|
| 甄士隐 | 甄士隐 | Zhen Shiyin | Чжэнь Шиинь | 甄士隠（しんしいん） | Pun: 真事隐 (true events hidden) |
| 贾雨村 | 贾雨村 | Jia Yucun | Цзя Юйцунь | 賈雨村（かうそん） | Pun: 假语存 (false words remain) |
| 英莲 | 英莲 | Yinglian | Инлянь | 英蓮（えいれん） | Pun: 应怜 (should be pitied) |

❌ **Don't translate names literally**:
- NOT "True Events Hidden" for 甄士隐
- NOT "False Words Remain" for 贾雨村

✅ **Do explain the pun in notes**:
```json
"notes": [
  "甄士隐 (Zhen Shiyin) is a pun on 真事隐 (true events hidden), suggesting the author conceals real people behind fictional names."
]
```

### 2. Classical Particles and Grammar

**Challenge**: Classical Chinese uses particles (之、乎、者、也) that don't translate directly.

**Approach**: Translate the meaning and tone, not word-for-word.

**Example**:

**Original**: 此开卷第一回也。

❌ **Bad literal**: This open scroll first chapter is.

✅ **Good**: This is the first chapter of the opening.

**Explanation**: The final 也 is a classical assertion particle. In English, we use word order and "is" to convey assertion.

### 3. Cultural Context

**Rule**: When a concept has no direct equivalent, translate + add note.

**Example**:

**Original**: 贾政的荣禧堂悬着"万几宸翰之宝"御赐匾额

**Translation approach**:
- Translate the phrase
- Add note explaining the cultural significance

✅ **Good English**: 
"The Rongxi Hall of Jia Zheng displayed an imperial plaque with the seal 'Treasure of the Imperial Brush of Ten Thousand Affairs.'"

**Corresponding note**:
```json
"notes": [
  "万几宸翰之宝: A seal indicating the Emperor himself wrote the plaque, showing the Jia family's high imperial favor. 'Ten Thousand Affairs' refers to the Emperor's governance responsibilities."
]
```

## Common Failures and Fixes

### Failure 1: Incomplete Page Translation

**Problem**: Translator translates 3 segments when page has 8 visible segments.

**Fix**: Before saving, re-read the original page and count segments visually.

**Checklist**:
- [ ] Every paragraph translated?
- [ ] Every line of poetry translated?
- [ ] Every speech translated?
- [ ] Every commentary note translated?

### Failure 2: Missing Commentary

**Problem**: Main text translated, but all commentary skipped.

**Why this happens**: Commentary is in smaller font, harder to read.

**Fix**: Deliberately look for commentary:
- Check page margins (眉批/侧批)
- Check between lines (夹批)
- Check after paragraphs
- Look for 【甲戌】【庚辰】markers

### Failure 3: Over-Modernization

**Problem**: Classical Chinese translated into overly casual modern language.

**Example**:

Original: 士隐见女儿越发生得粉妆玉琢

❌ **Too modern**: 士隐看到女儿越来越漂亮了

✅ **Appropriate**: 士隐见女儿越发长得粉雕玉琢

**Explanation**: 
- Keep "粉雕玉琢" (carved from pink jade) - it's a classical phrase but understandable
- Don't reduce to "漂亮" (pretty) - too generic

### Failure 4: Low-Quality Research Notes

**Problem**: Notes are too generic or state the obvious.

❌ **Bad notes**:
```json
"notes": [
  "This page is from Chapter 1.",
  "There is poetry on this page.",
  "Characters appear in this scene."
]
```

**Why bad**: States obvious facts, adds no value.

✅ **Good notes**:
```json
"notes": [
  "菱花: Puns on 英莲's later name 香菱, and also means 'mirror' (菱花镜), suggesting vanity/impermanence.",
  "元宵 (Lantern Festival): Foreshadows Yinglian's kidnapping, which occurs during the Lantern Festival.",
  "癞头和尚/跛足道人: These supernatural figures appear throughout the novel as enlightened beings whom worldly characters cannot recognize."
]
```

**Why good**: 
- Non-obvious insights
- Explains symbolism
- Shows research depth
- Adds scholarly value

### Failure 5: Inconsistent Translation Choices

**Problem**: Same term translated differently across segments.

**Example**:

Segment 1: 贾宝玉 → "Jia Baoyu"  
Segment 5: 贾宝玉 → "Precious Jade Jia"

**Fix**: Use `research/glossary.md` to ensure consistency.

**Standard translations must be maintained**:
- 贾宝玉 = Jia Baoyu (English/Russian transliteration)
- 林黛玉 = Lin Daiyu
- 红楼梦 = Dream of the Red Chamber (or The Story of the Stone)

## Self-Evaluation Questions

Before marking a page "complete," ask yourself:

### Completeness
1. Did I translate every visible text element?
2. Did I translate every commentary note?
3. Are there any blank spaces in my translations?

### Quality
4. Would a scholar respect these translations?
5. Do the translations feel literary, not mechanical?
6. Did I preserve the emotional tone?

### Accuracy
7. Are all four translations saying the same thing?
8. Did I check difficult passages against scholarly sources?
9. Did I explain cultural concepts that need context?

### Research
10. Did I document non-obvious findings in `notes`?
11. Did I look up allusions and symbolism?
12. Did I spend adequate time on each segment?

**If any answer is "no" or "unsure," revise before saving.**

## Time Investment Expectations

For reference, a well-done page should take:

- **Simple page** (minimal commentary, straightforward prose): 30-45 minutes
- **Average page** (some commentary, mixture of prose/dialogue): 45-75 minutes  
- **Complex page** (heavy commentary, poetry, dense allusions): 90-120 minutes

**If you're completing pages in <20 minutes**, you're probably rushing and sacrificing quality.

**If you're taking >2 hours per page**, you may be overthinking. Time-box difficult passages and add uncertain notes.
